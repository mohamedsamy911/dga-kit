# Raw capture — component and template re-read, 2026-10-18

Evidence for the 2026-10-18 re-read of every component and template page against the kit.

## Method

DGA's page text is an English i18n table inside its live bundle `assets/index-BO_6JOKP.js`
(unchanged since 2026-10-08). The table was extracted verbatim — 176 of 176 sampled values
matched the bundle byte for byte — and two reviewers compared it with the kit. Every passage
below was then confirmed **displayed** on the rendered English page (34 pages rendered in a
browser on 2026-10-18): the table also holds strings DGA never shows, such as a "Selected" state
for Input, Switch and Textarea and per-level percentage bands on the Assessment Criteria page,
and those are not evidence. Passages are DGA i18n values with markup stripped.

## `/guidelines/components/data-display/content-switcher`

<!-- dga -->
> The appearance of a content switcher typically includes a set of options or tabs arranged horizontally. Each option represents a different piece of content or view that users can switch between. The option limits for content switchers range from two to four. Having less than two options would render a content switcher useless, while having more than four could clutter and complicate the interface. Tabs are a better choice for managing larger amounts of options, as they provide a more organized and clear method to display multiple categories or sections

## `/guidelines/components/loading-and-status/skeleton`

<!-- dga -->
> Displays a circular placeholder typically used for profile pictures, icons, or logos in user interfaces. With multiple size options between 24px to 240px to fit different layouts.
>
> Displays a rectangular placeholder commonly used for form fields, feature highlights, or descriptive content, with multiple size options like large, medium, and small, and widths like short and long to fit different layouts.
>
> Displays stacked line placeholders ideal for text-based content such as articles, bullet points, or lists.With multiple size options like small, and large to fit different layouts.
>
> Minimize animations, or keep them subtle (e.g., gentle pulsing) to avoid overwhelming users, especially those with vestibular disorders or sensitivities to motion. Use the prefers-reduced-motion media query to disable animations for users who have requested it in their system preferences. If animations are used, provide an option to pause them or disable them entirely to accommodate user needs and improve accessibility.
>
> Skeleton Loaders should not receive keyboard focus to prevent users from accidentally navigating to loading elements. Apply tabindex="-1" to ensure these elements are skipped in the tab order. Provide clear focus indicators on the content elements once they load, helping keyboard users reorient themselves within the new content.
>
> Add aria-label or aria-describedby to Skeleton Loaders to provide additional context, such as "Content loading, please wait." This helps users who rely on screen readers understand what the skeleton represents, preventing confusion with static content. Ensure that, when loading completes, the Skeleton Loader is removed from the accessibility tree (using aria-hidden="true" or dynamically removing the elements) so that it doesn’t interfere with screen reader navigation after the content has loaded.
